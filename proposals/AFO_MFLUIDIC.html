Project Description:

Simulating the mechanical behavior and flow characteristics of biological fluids is a cutting-edge research endeavor in modern engineering. This project focuses on the design and simulation of a microfluidic blood filtration chip. By combining surface-acoustic wave based piezoelectric biosensor technology with specific-molecule targeting aptamer synthesis techniques, the project aims to develop micro-channel geometries that passively concentrate particles towards the aptamer regions by purely mechanical design. Given that solving this complex fluid-structure interaction and multiphase flow problem requires rigorous numerical analysis, the project offers an ideal platform to employ computer-aided engineering (CAE) and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) software. The workflow involves developing parametric CAD models and running iterative simulations to optimize the channel geometries, making it a comprehensive exercise in both academic research and applied biomechanics.

Expected Work:

  1. Conduct a comprehensive literature review on blood rheology, aptamer sythesis for particle targeting, microfluidics, and passive mechanical separation techniques.
  2. Design a script-based and parametric microfluidics channel geometry which naturally yields to a CFD based optimization procedure.
  3. Review the current metrics used in literature to identify concentration of particles based on their sizes and/or masses using CFD results.
  4. Perform Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations to analyze the flow field, fluid-structure interaction, and particle trajectories.
  5. Prepare a final engineering report detailing the methodology, simulation findings, and potential biomedical applications.
