Title: DEVELOPMENT OF A DATA-DRIVEN MODEL FOR TREND ANOMALY DETECTION IN VIBRATION DATA

Ref Code: VITAD (Acronym)

Type: Individual

Status: New

Vacancy: 1 student (two-semester project)

Project Description:

Condition monitoring of rotating machinery relies heavily on vibration measurements. A developing fault rarely appears as a single abrupt event; instead, it reveals itself as a slow drift in the statistical or spectral character of the signal long before a threshold-based alarm is triggered. The aim of this project is to develop a data-driven model that detects such trend anomalies by taking the previous behaviour of the vibration data into account. Rather than working directly on the raw time series, the model will operate on derived representations such as frequency spectra, spectral band energies, envelope spectra and statistical distributions, so that changes in the underlying trend can be identified even when the time-domain amplitude remains apparently unchanged. The work will cover signal pre-processing and feature extraction, construction of a suitable model (statistical change-point methods, regression/forecasting-residual approaches or machine learning architectures), and validation of the detection performance on real industrial data.

This project is carried out within the framework of a university-industry collaboration with Bias Muhendislik, who will provide the industrial vibration data sets used in the study. The project is planned as a two-semester study, and an application to the TUBITAK 2209-B undergraduate research support programme will be prepared as part of the work.

Work Description:

  1. Literature survey on vibration-based condition monitoring and anomaly/change-point detection methods.

  2. Familiarization with the industrial data set provided by Bias Muhendislik; data cleaning, segmentation and labelling.

  3. Extraction of time-domain, frequency-domain and statistical features; construction of feature trends over time.

  4. Development and calibration of data-driven models for trend anomaly detection on these representations.

  5. Performance evaluation (detection delay, false alarm rate) and comparison against classical baseline methods.

  6. Preparation of the TUBITAK 2209-B proposal, and reporting/presentation of the final results.

Qualifications and Skills:

  2. Background in vibrations and signal processing (FFT, filtering, spectral analysis).

  3. Interest in data analysis and data-driven modelling.

  4. Ability to work in coordination with an industrial partner and to commit to a two-semester study.
