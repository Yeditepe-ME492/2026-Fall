Title: DETERMINATION OF THE RELATIVE POSITION AND ORIENTATION BETWEEN TWO SENSORS USING APRILTAG OR SIMILAR FIDUCIAL MARKERS

Ref Code: TAGCAL (Acronym)

Type: Individual

Status: New

Vacancy: 1 student (two-semester project)

Project Description:

When two or more sensors observe the same scene, their measurements can only be fused if the rigid-body transformation between them is known. In practice this extrinsic calibration is often obtained by manual measurement, which is slow, operator-dependent and difficult to repeat whenever a sensor is moved or replaced. The aim of this project is to determine the relative position and orientation of two sensors automatically by means of fiducial markers such as AprilTag, ArUco or equivalent targets. The marker is observed by both sensors, the individual measurements are expressed in a common reference frame, and the X-Y-Z position differences together with the roll, pitch and yaw angles between the sensors are computed from the resulting pose estimates. The work includes intrinsic camera calibration, marker pose estimation, formulation and solution of the rigid-body transformation problem, averaging over multiple marker poses to reduce noise, and an assessment of the accuracy and repeatability of the estimated transformation against reference measurements.

This project is carried out within the framework of a university-industry collaboration with Bias Muhendislik, who will provide the sensors and the measurement data used in the study. The project is planned as a two-semester study, and an application to the TUBITAK 2209-B undergraduate research support programme will be prepared as part of the work.

Work Description:

  1. Literature survey on fiducial marker systems (AprilTag, ArUco, ChArUco) and extrinsic sensor calibration methods.

  2. Review of rigid-body kinematics: homogeneous transformations, rotation representations and Euler angle conventions.

  3. Intrinsic calibration of the sensors and implementation of marker detection and pose estimation.

  4. Formulation and solution of the sensor-to-sensor transformation; estimation of X-Y-Z offsets and roll, pitch, yaw angles.

  5. Experimental verification: accuracy, repeatability and sensitivity to marker distance, viewing angle and lighting conditions.

  6. Preparation of the TUBITAK 2209-B proposal, and reporting/presentation of the final results.
